# The Launchpad Framework

## Purpose

The Launchpad is the strategic foundation of every presentation. It ensures you've done the hard thinking BEFORE creating any slides. Most presentation failures can be traced to skipping or rushing this phase.

> "Give me six hours to chop down a tree and I will spend the first four sharpening the axe." — Abraham Lincoln

## 1. Audience Definition

### Why It Matters

The same information, presented differently, can inspire or bore. The difference is audience alignment.

### Questions to Answer

| Question | Why It Matters |
|----------|----------------|
| Who is in the room? | Determines technical depth and vocabulary |
| What do they already know? | Avoids boring them or losing them |
| What do they believe? | Reveals resistance points |
| What do they care about? | Determines what to emphasize |
| What are they afraid of? | Addresses objections proactively |
| What decision can they make? | Focuses the ask |

### Audience Profile Template

```
AUDIENCE: [Name/Role]
─────────────────────────────────────────────
KNOWS:      What they already understand
BELIEVES:   Their current assumptions/biases
CARES ABOUT: Their priorities and concerns
FEARS:      What keeps them up at night
CAN DECIDE: What authority they have
NEEDS:      What they need from this presentation
```

### Example

```
AUDIENCE: Executive Committee (CEO, CFO, COO, CTO)
─────────────────────────────────────────────
KNOWS:      Our revenue has plateaued
BELIEVES:   Cost-cutting is the safest path
CARES ABOUT: Shareholder value, risk mitigation
FEARS:      Making a bet that fails publicly
CAN DECIDE: Approve investments up to $50M
NEEDS:      Confidence that this is the right move
```

## 2. SCQA Framework

### The Four Elements

**S - Situation**
The starting point that everyone agrees on. Establishes common ground and credibility.

**C - Complication**
What has changed, what's at risk, or what problem has emerged. Creates tension and urgency.

**Q - Question**
The specific question that this presentation will answer. Focuses the discussion.

**A - Answer (The Big Idea)**
Your response to the question. The core message in one sentence.

### The Logic Flow

```
SITUATION        "We all know that..."
     │
     ▼
COMPLICATION     "However, something has changed..."
     │
     ▼
QUESTION         "This raises the question..."
     │
     ▼
ANSWER           "Our answer is..."
```

### SCQA Examples

**Example 1: Growth Strategy**
```
S: We've achieved 15% CAGR for the past 5 years through organic growth.
C: Market maturation means organic growth will slow to 3-5% annually.
Q: How do we maintain double-digit growth?
A: We must pursue strategic acquisitions in adjacent markets.
```

**Example 2: Digital Transformation**
```
S: Our company has been the industry leader for 20 years.
C: Digital-native competitors are taking share from us at an accelerating rate.
Q: How do we respond before it's too late?
A: We must transform our business model around digital-first experiences.
```

**Example 3: Operational Issue**
```
S: Our customer satisfaction scores have historically been above 85%.
C: In the last two quarters, scores dropped to 72%, and churn is increasing.
Q: What's driving the decline and how do we reverse it?
A: Our service delivery process is broken—we need to redesign it around
   customer journey moments that matter.
```

### The Big Idea Formula

```
[Your Point of View] + [What's at Stake] = Big Idea
```

**Requirements:**
- ≤ 20 words
- States a point of view (not just a topic)
- Conveys stakes (why it matters)
- Memorable and repeatable

**Testing Your Big Idea:**
- If someone asks "what was that presentation about?" would the audience answer with your Big Idea?
- Could it be a tweet? A headline? A bumper sticker?
- Does it make someone want to know more?

## 3. The 3 Pillars

### Why Three?

Three is the magic number for human cognition:
- Easy to remember
- Creates completeness (beginning, middle, end)
- Allows for nuance without overwhelm
- Works across cultures and contexts

### Pillar Definition

Each pillar represents a major supporting argument for your Answer. Together, they must be:
- **MECE:** No overlaps, no gaps
- **Persuasive:** Each addresses a key concern
- **Logical:** They naturally support the Big Idea

### Pillar Template

```
BIG IDEA: [Your Answer]

PILLAR 1: [What they must believe #1]
└─► Addresses: [What concern/objection]
└─► Evidence: [What proof you have]

PILLAR 2: [What they must believe #2]
└─► Addresses: [What concern/objection]
└─► Evidence: [What proof you have]

PILLAR 3: [What they must believe #3]
└─► Addresses: [What concern/objection]
└─► Evidence: [What proof you have]
```

### Example

```
BIG IDEA: "We should acquire TechCo to accelerate our AI capabilities."

PILLAR 1: Strategic Fit
└─► Addresses: "Is this the right target?"
└─► Evidence: Capability mapping, market position, synergies

PILLAR 2: Financial Merit
└─► Addresses: "Is the price right?"
└─► Evidence: Valuation analysis, ROI projections, funding options

PILLAR 3: Execution Feasibility
└─► Addresses: "Can we actually do this?"
└─► Evidence: Integration plan, risk mitigation, team capabilities
```

### Common Pillar Patterns

| Pattern | Pillar 1 | Pillar 2 | Pillar 3 |
|---------|----------|----------|----------|
| The Case | Why this is a problem | Why our solution works | Why now |
| The Analysis | What's happening | Why it's happening | What to do |
| The Proposal | The opportunity | Our approach | The ask |
| The Update | What we did | What we learned | What's next |
| The Vision | Where we are | Where we're going | How we get there |

## 4. Block Mapping

### From Pillars to Blocks

Each pillar typically maps to 1-3 blocks:

```
PILLAR 1: Strategic Fit
├── Block 1.1: Market Opportunity (why the market matters)
├── Block 1.2: TechCo's Position (why this specific target)
└── Block 1.3: Synergy Analysis (why 1+1=3)

PILLAR 2: Financial Merit
├── Block 2.1: Valuation (what it's worth)
└── Block 2.2: Returns (what we get back)

PILLAR 3: Execution Feasibility
├── Block 3.1: Integration Plan (how we do it)
└── Block 3.2: Risk Mitigation (how we protect ourselves)
```

### Block Sizing Guide

| Presentation Length | Blocks | Slides per Block |
|---------------------|--------|------------------|
| 15 minutes | 3-4 | 4-6 |
| 30 minutes | 5-7 | 5-8 |
| 45 minutes | 7-9 | 6-10 |
| 60 minutes | 9-12 | 6-10 |

### Block Structure Template

```
BLOCK: [Block Title]
─────────────────────────────────────────────
PURPOSE:     What this block proves
KEY CLAIM:   The one thing the audience should believe
EVIDENCE:    What data/proof supports this
LOOPS:       How many sub-arguments (typically 2-4)
DURATION:    Estimated time in presentation
```

## 5. Success Criteria

### Defining Success

Before building slides, define what success looks like:

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| What decision do we need? | [Specific decision] |
| What's the minimum viable outcome? | [Fallback position] |
| What would "home run" look like? | [Best case scenario] |
| How will we know we succeeded? | [Observable indicators] |

### Example

```
DECISION NEEDED: Approval to proceed with TechCo acquisition due diligence

MINIMUM VIABLE: Committee agrees this is worth exploring further

HOME RUN: Committee approves immediate DD with $500K budget

SUCCESS INDICATORS:
- Questions focus on "how" not "whether"
- CFO asks about funding mechanics
- CEO says "let's move forward"
```

## Launchpad Checklist

Before moving to Block-level work, verify:

### Audience
- [ ] I know exactly who will be in the room
- [ ] I understand what they care about
- [ ] I know what objections they'll have
- [ ] I know what decision they can make

### SCQA
- [ ] Situation establishes common ground
- [ ] Complication creates appropriate tension
- [ ] Question is specific and answerable
- [ ] Answer is clear and in one sentence

### Big Idea
- [ ] States a point of view (not just a topic)
- [ ] Conveys stakes (why it matters)
- [ ] Is ≤ 20 words
- [ ] Is memorable

### Pillars
- [ ] Three pillars that are MECE
- [ ] Each addresses a key concern
- [ ] Each has supporting evidence
- [ ] Together they fully support the Big Idea

### Blocks
- [ ] Each pillar maps to 1-3 blocks
- [ ] Total block count fits the time available
- [ ] Each block has a clear purpose

### Success
- [ ] I know what decision I'm seeking
- [ ] I know what minimum success looks like
- [ ] I know how I'll recognize success

## Common Launchpad Mistakes

1. **Skipping It:** Jumping straight to slides
2. **Vague Audience:** "Everyone" or "leadership"
3. **Weak Complication:** Not enough tension to care
4. **Topic vs. Point of View:** "AI strategy" instead of "AI is existential for us"
5. **Four+ Pillars:** Too many to remember
6. **Overlapping Pillars:** Not MECE
7. **Unclear Success:** Not knowing what decision you need
